* Typing and where a second space goes

What =garamond-mode= does as you type, and where it leaves the text alone.

** The mode

=M-x garamond-mode= makes one space typed after a sentence's end two, or stops
doing so. It acts only while the buffer's sentences are two spaces apart. The
mode line says =2S= then. While they are one apart it says =1S=, and the mode
waits. It also acts only in prose, and never after an abbreviation or a list
item's number. A run of more spaces typed is made two.

A count turns it on when it is positive and off when it is zero or less. It is
turned on by itself in a file that says its spacing, and by
=garamond-set-spacing= and =garamond-adjust-spacing=.

=M-x garamond-follow-declarations-mode= turns that starting by itself on or
off, for files opened after it. Its setting, =*garamond-follow-declarations*=,
is on when the package is loaded.

#+table: commands garamond-mode garamond-follow-declarations-mode

** Where a second space goes

Only where prose is written:

- in a text mode (Org, Markdown, plain text and the rest), never in code,
  tables, links, maths or a block's contents. Org's parse, or the language's
  syntax tree, tells which is which;
- in any other language, only in its comments.

Where a language has a syntax tree that is not ready yet, nothing is doubled.

** Abbreviations

A word whose full stop shortens it (for example Dr. or Sept.) ends no sentence.
The abbreviations are Tuatara's own, the ones its sentence commands and filling
pass over. Add your own with =*extra-sentence-abbreviations*= and take any of
Tuatara's out with =*removed-sentence-abbreviations*=, both in the init file.
Chapter 2 of Tuatara's manual covers the sentence commands.

** What a sentence's end is

A letter, then =.=, =?=, =!=, =…= or =‽=, then any closing quotes or brackets.
A list item's number (1. or 10.) ends no sentence, because no letter comes
before its full stop.
